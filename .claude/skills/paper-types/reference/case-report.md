# Case Report

## CARE Checklist (13 Items)

The CARE (Case Report Guidelines) checklist is the standard for case reports.

| # | Item | Content | Location |
|----|------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Case report title | Title includes "case report" or "case series"; identifies unique/instructive feature | Title page |
| 2 | Keywords | 3–5 relevant terms for indexing | Title page |
| 3 | Case demographics | Patient age, gender, ethnicity, occupation, relevant background | Case presentation |
| 4 | Case history | Relevant past medical history, medications, allergies, social/family history | Case presentation |
| 5 | Timeline of clinical events | Chronological sequence: symptom onset, investigations, interventions, outcomes (include dates) | Case presentation |
| 6 | Clinical findings | Presenting complaint, vital signs, physical exam findings, relevant negative findings | Case presentation |
| 7 | Diagnostic tests | Tests performed, results with normal ranges, imaging findings, etc. | Case presentation |
| 8 | Diagnostic reasoning | Why was diagnosis considered? How was it confirmed? What was ruled out? | Case presentation or Discussion |
| 9 | Intervention | Treatment(s) provided: drug names, doses, routes, duration; procedures; supportive care | Case presentation |
| 10 | Outcome and follow-up | Clinical resolution or current status; follow-up period; functional status at last follow-up | Case presentation or Results |
| 11 | Informed consent | Written informed consent obtained (date); if not obtainable, why (deceased, lost contact) and how privacy was ensured | Methods or Results |
| 12 | Discussion | What is unusual/novel about this case? How does it fit literature? Differential diagnoses considered? Learning points? | Discussion |
| 13 | Limitations | Limitations (single case, recall bias, lack of imaging, etc.) and how they affect the conclusions | Discussion |

## Timeline Requirement (CRITICAL)

One of the hallmarks of a good case report is a **clear, detailed timeline**. Readers need to track what happened when.

### Timeline presentation formats

**Option 1: Narrative with embedded dates**
> "The patient first presented to the emergency department on March 15, 2023, complaining of chest pain. On March 16, she underwent an ECG (findings: ST elevation). By March 18, troponin levels had normalized and she was discharged with a diagnosis of atypical angina. Follow-up on April 2, 2023, showed complete resolution of symptoms."

**Option 2: Table format** (clearer for complex cases)

| Date | Event | Clinical Finding | Intervention |
|------|-------|------------------|-------------|
| Mar 15, 2023 | ED presentation | Chest pain, BP 140/90, HR 95 | Supportive care ordered |
| Mar 16, 2023 | Hospitalization | ECG: ST elevation | Cardiac monitoring started |
| Mar 17, 2023 | Laboratory | Troponin 0.05 (ref: <0.04) | Aspirin 325 mg daily |
| Mar 18, 2023 | Follow-up labs | Troponin normalized | Discharged with diagnosis |
| Apr 2, 2023 | Clinic follow-up | Asymptomatic, normal vitals | Continued outpatient care |

**Option 3: Timeline figure** (visual representation useful for complex cases)

```
Symptom onset (date)
        ↓
First medical contact (date)
        ↓
Diagnostic tests (dates & results)
        ↓
Intervention initiated (date & type)
        ↓
Outcome/resolution (date & status)
```

### What the timeline must include

- **Symptom onset:** When did the patient first notice the problem?
- **First medical contact:** When did they seek care?
- **Investigation sequence:** Each diagnostic test date & result.
- **Intervention dates:** When was treatment started?
- **Outcome milestones:** When did symptoms resolve/progress?
- **Follow-up:** Date of last contact; current status.

## Consent & Privacy Requirements (LEGAL)

Case reports involve identifiable patient information. You MUST address:

### Informed consent statement (MANDATORY)

Include in Methods or Results:
> "Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication of this case report on [DATE]. A copy of the written consent is available upon request from the corresponding author."

**If consent cannot be obtained:**
- Patient deceased: "The patient passed away on [DATE]; next of kin consent was obtained on [DATE]."
- Lost contact: Explain: "Despite reasonable efforts to locate the patient (attempted contact via [method], [date]), we were unable to obtain consent. However, the case has been de-identified as described below."

### De-identification measures

- **Remove/mask:** Patient names, hospital identifiers, medical record numbers, specific dates (use "Day 1," "Day 2," or month/year only), geographic details (if identifiable).
- **Retain:** Age, gender, clinical features necessary for the case (other details anonymized).
- Example: "A 67-year-old female with type 2 diabetes presented to a large teaching hospital in 2023 with…" (no name, specific hospital, or exact dates given).

## Structure & Content Guidance

### Introduction
- **Significance:** Why is this case worth reporting? Is it rare? Does it challenge existing knowledge? Is it a new presentation or complication?
- **Gap:** What in the literature prompted you to publish this? (e.g., "Although X has been reported, this is the first case of X combined with Y in this patient population.")
- Length: 2–3 paragraphs.

### Case presentation
- **Patient background:** Demographics, occupation, relevant medical history (past diagnoses, medications, allergies, family history).
- **Presenting complaint & history:** What brought the patient to medical attention? Duration and evolution of symptoms.
- **Clinical examination:** Vital signs, physical findings, systems review, abnormalities.
- **Investigations:** Lab work, imaging, specialized tests (results with reference ranges, abnormal findings flagged).
- **Timeline:** Embed dates or use a table.
- Length: 3–4 pages (detailed but concise).

### Diagnosis / Clinical course
- **How was diagnosis made?** Were there diagnostic challenges? Differential diagnoses considered and ruled out?
- **Investigations that clinched it:** Which finding was diagnostic?
- **Outcome:** How did the patient respond to treatment? Resolution, improvement, ongoing management?

### Discussion
- **Significance:** Reiterate why this case matters (comparison to prior published cases, if any).
- **Mechanism:** If known, explain the underlying pathophysiology or how this case challenges or confirms existing theory.
- **Learning point:** What should clinicians/readers know from this case? (e.g., "Consider X in patients with Y, even without Z.")
- **Limitations:** Single case means limited generalizability; recall bias if history from patient; lack of certain investigations if unavailable.
- **Future directions:** What would help clarify this phenomenon? Are case series or mechanism studies warranted?
- Length: 2–3 pages.

### Conclusion
- One-sentence summary of key learning.

## Word Budget

**Typical case report:** 2000–3000 words (strict limit for most case-report journals).
- Introduction: 300–400 words
- Case presentation: 1000–1200 words
- Discussion: 600–900 words
- Conclusion: 100–150 words

**Case series** (multiple related cases): 3000–4000 words; same structure, but synthesize findings across cases.

## Unique Challenges

### Single case = limited evidence

- **Acknowledge:** "This is a single case; findings are not generalizable."
- **Offset:** Explain why this case is instructive anyway (rare, novel combination, challenges theory, teaches mechanism).

### Recall bias

- **If relying on patient memory:** "Information on past medical history relied on patient recall; medical records were not available for [year range]."
- **Mitigate:** Use available documentation; be explicit about what's verified vs. recollected.

### Ethical issues (if applicable)

- **Off-label use:** If the intervention was off-label, state: "The patient received [drug] off-label because [reason]. Informed consent for off-label use was obtained."
- **Unusual procedure:** "Ethics approval was obtained from the IRB on [date] for this novel procedure."

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| No timeline (events in unclear sequence) | Add dates throughout; use a table if complex |
| Missing consent statement (legally risky) | Always include: "Written consent was obtained on [DATE]" or explain why not |
| No de-identification (privacy breach risk) | Remove names, MRN, specific hospital/location, exact dates (use "Day 1" or month/year) |
| Vague clinical findings ("Patient was unwell") | Specific: "Temperature 39.2°C, BP 142/88, HR 105; patient appeared distressed" |
| No reference range for labs (readers can't assess abnormality) | Include: "Hemoglobin 9.2 g/dL (reference: 12.0–16.0)" |
| Diagnosis not explained (why you think it's X, not Y) | Explain reasoning: diagnostic test results, exclusion of other diagnoses |
| Discussion too long (reads like a mini review) | Focus on THIS case and its implications; avoid extended literature review |
| No limitations section (presents case as definitive) | Acknowledge single-case limitation; explain why it's still publishable |

## Why Write a Case Report?

- **Raise awareness** of rare presentations or complications.
- **Report new side effects** or drug reactions.
- **Describe an innovative procedure** or treatment approach.
- **Challenge conventional wisdom** with a counter-example.
- **Generate a hypothesis** for future research.

Ask: "Would a clinician benefit from knowing about this case? Does it add to collective knowledge?"
