# Rubric: Empirical Research (IMRaD)

Used by `reviewer-critic` to score IMRaD papers (empirical studies, RCTs, observational, case reports) on journal-grade dimensions. Each dimension scored 1–5; Major issues (1–2) must be resolved before publication.

---

## Dimension 1: Contribution Clarity & Novelty (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the research question clear? Is the contribution novel and significant?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Research question is crystal clear, stated as hypothesis or explicit RQ. Contribution is novel (extends existing methods, first in domain, solves known gap). Significance is immediate (e.g., will affect practice, theory, or large population). |
| **4** | RQ is clear and stated explicitly. Contribution is solid and novel. Significance is stated but could be stronger. |
| **3** | RQ is stated but somewhat vague or buried. Contribution is incremental or clear but marginal. Significance unclear or overstated. |
| **2** | RQ is vague or implied; reader must infer it. Contribution is unclear or appears to duplicate existing work. Significance not justified. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No clear RQ. No discernible contribution or it is clearly not novel. Significance unknown or irrelevant. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Is there a one-sentence RQ or hypothesis in Introduction?
- If prior work has done similar work, why is this different/better?
- Is significance justified (new population, unexpected result, practical impact, theoretical advancement)?

---

## Dimension 2: Argument Integrity – Claim-Evidence Chain (1–5)

**Definition:** Are claims supported by evidence? Do conclusions follow from results? Is the logic transparent?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Every major claim in Introduction, Results, and Discussion is directly supported by cited evidence or data. RQ stated in Intro ↔ addressed in Results ↔ answered in Discussion. No logical gaps. |
| **4** | Claims are mostly well-supported. Minor unsupported assertions, but overall logic is sound. |
| **3** | Some claims lack support or evidence is weak. RQ-Results-Discussion chain is present but loose. Reader must infer some connections. |
| **2** | Multiple claims unsupported by evidence. RQ and results don't clearly align. Logic has gaps. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Claims appear arbitrary or contradicted by data. RQ unrelated to Results. Incoherent argument. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Trace one major claim from Intro → Results → Discussion: is it answered?
- Do Discussion claims cite specific Results, or are they vague generalities?
- Are alternative explanations for results considered?
- Are limitations acknowledged (strengthens honesty, not weakness)?

---

## Dimension 3: Methods-Results-Conclusion Coherence (1–5)

**Definition:** Are Methods sufficiently detailed to evaluate Results? Do Results answer the Methods? Is Conclusion justified by Results?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Methods are detailed (replicable). Results directly address hypotheses/RQ stated in Introduction. Conclusion re-states key finding(s) and does not overreach. |
| **4** | Methods are adequate; some detail could be added. Results mostly address RQ. Conclusion is justified. |
| **3** | Methods lack some key detail (replicability uncertain). Results address RQ but incompletely. Conclusion somewhat overstates findings. |
| **2** | Methods vague or incomplete (replication difficult). Results partially address RQ or include tangential findings. Conclusion makes unsupported jumps. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Methods missing critical information. Results and RQ unrelated. Conclusion contradicts or ignores Results. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Could another researcher replicate this study from Methods?
- Is sample size justified (power analysis, prior literature)?
- Are all statistical details reported (N, df, p, CI, effect size)?
- Does Conclusion address the specific RQ, or is it generic?

---

## Dimension 4: Positioning vs. Literature (1–5)

**Definition:** Is prior work accurately cited? Does the paper clearly show where it fits (gap-filling, theory extension, novel application)?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Literature review is current and accurate. Prior work organized thematically (not just chronologically). Gap is clearly identified (gap in population, method, context, theory). Paper's contribution is explicit relative to the gap. |
| **4** | Literature review is solid and mostly current. Gap is identified but could be clearer. Positioning is clear. |
| **3** | Literature review present but may be incomplete or superficial. Gap implied but not explicit. Positioning unclear. |
| **2** | Literature review missing key references or contains errors. Gap vague. Positioning unclear or overstated (e.g., "first ever" when similar work exists). **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Inadequate literature review. Gap is not identified. Positioning unknown. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Are key papers in the field cited (last 5 years)?
- Is prior work organized conceptually, not as a list?
- Is the gap (what is NOT known) stated in 1–2 sentences?
- Does the paper address that specific gap, or something else?

---

## Dimension 5: Quality of Comparisons (Baselines, Criteria, Trade-offs) (1–5)

**Definition:** If comparing to prior methods/theories, are comparisons fair? Are criteria for comparison explicit? Are trade-offs addressed?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | All comparisons are fair (same conditions, sample, timeframe, etc.). Comparison criteria stated explicitly before comparison. Trade-offs acknowledged (e.g., "Method A is faster but less accurate than B"). No cherry-picking. |
| **4** | Comparisons are generally fair. Criteria mostly explicit. Trade-offs mentioned. |
| **3** | Comparisons present but fairness unclear. Criteria implicit. Trade-offs not discussed. |
| **2** | Comparisons lack key details (different conditions, unclear criteria). Cherry-picked results. Trade-offs ignored. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No comparison or entirely unfair (incomparable samples, hidden criteria). Selective reporting. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- If comparing to a prior method, were the methods applied to the same data/sample?
- Are effect sizes or practical significance reported, not just p-values?
- Are limitations of the comparison acknowledged (e.g., "Prior method was designed for different population; direct comparison is imperfect")?
- Are all comparisons reported, or only favorable ones?

---

## Dimension 6: Abstract ↔ Body Consistency (1–5)

**Definition:** Do claims in the abstract match the Results and Conclusion? Is the abstract informative and accurate?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Abstract accurately summarizes problem, method, key results, and conclusion. No claims in abstract unsupported by body. Abstract is concrete (not vague). |
| **4** | Abstract is mostly accurate and matches body. Minor inconsistencies. |
| **3** | Abstract has some unsupported claims or vague descriptions. Reader would need to read full paper to verify abstract's claims. |
| **2** | Abstract makes claims not supported in body, or body contradicts abstract. Vague or misleading. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Abstract is mostly false or completely unrelated to body. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Abstract claims a result; does the Results section report it?
- Abstract uses vague language ("promising," "improved") without numbers; does body provide specifics?
- Does abstract overstate or understate significance compared to body?

---

## Dimension 7: Title Fit (1–5)

**Definition:** Does the title accurately reflect the paper's content, question, and contribution?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Title is specific, accurately describes the content, and implies the contribution. Researcher unfamiliar with field would understand the paper's topic. |
| **4** | Title is clear and accurate. Minor vagueness. |
| **3** | Title is general or somewhat vague. Reader's expectation might not match content. |
| **2** | Title is misleading or doesn't reflect contribution. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Title is unrelated to content. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- If you read only the title, would you expect the paper's actual content?
- Does title claim a finding not supported in the paper?
- Example of good title: "Deep Learning Outperforms Traditional Methods for Bearing Fault Diagnosis Under Noisy Conditions" (specific).
- Example of bad title: "An Application of Neural Networks" (too generic).

---

## Dimension 8: Honesty of Limitations (1–5)

**Definition:** Are limitations acknowledged? Are they plausible and not dismissive?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Limitations section clearly identifies scope limits (sample, setting, measurement, design). Acknowledges how each limits conclusions (e.g., "sample was predominantly white, limiting generalizability to other populations"). Does not over-claim. |
| **4** | Limitations acknowledged. Scope is clear. Minor gaps in limitation discussion. |
| **3** | Limitations mentioned but brief or superficial. Some potential limitations not discussed. |
| **2** | Limitations vague or dismissive ("small sample but results are still important"). Or paper makes strong claims despite obvious limitations. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No limitations section or limitations are minimized/ignored despite being severe. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Does the Limitations section exist and occupy ≥1 paragraph?
- Are limitations tied to actual constraints (budget, access, time), not just wished-for perfection?
- Are there obvious limitations not mentioned (e.g., no randomization claimed as limitation for an observational study)?
- Do authors suggest future research to address limitations, or just list them?

---

## Dimension 9: Figure/Table Sufficiency (1–5)

**Definition:** Are figures and tables sufficient to understand key results? Are captions clear and self-contained?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Figures/tables are numerous and informative (≥1 per major finding). Captions are self-contained; reader understands figure without reading text. Labels are clear. Legends included where applicable. |
| **4** | Figures/tables present and mostly clear. Captions adequate. Minor improvements needed. |
| **3** | Figures/tables present but sparse or captions require reading text to understand. Some labels unclear. |
| **2** | Figures/tables missing for important results, or captions are vague ("Results are shown in Figure 1" without explanation). **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No figures/tables, or they are uninterpretable. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Can you understand the main finding from each figure/table caption alone?
- Are results tables formatted clearly (headers labeled, units shown, best results highlighted)?
- For descriptive studies, is there a table of sample characteristics?
- For statistical results, are means, SDs, and statistical tests (t, p, CI) reported?

---

## Dimension 10: Statistical Reporting Correctness (1–5)

**Definition:** Are statistical methods appropriate? Are results reported completely and correctly?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Statistical tests are appropriate for data (parametric/non-parametric, sample size, design). Results report: N, M, SD, test statistic (t, F, r), df, p-value, 95% CI, effect size (d, r, η²). No p-hacking apparent (α level pre-set, not fished). |
| **4** | Statistical tests appropriate. Results mostly complete (may lack CI or effect size but otherwise adequate). |
| **3** | Statistical tests seem reasonable but may have issues (e.g., parametric test on non-normal data). Results incomplete (p-value only, no effect size). |
| **2** | Statistical approach questionable or results incomplete (only p-values, no effect sizes or CIs). Evidence of p-hacking (many tests, no correction). **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Incorrect statistical tests or results not reported at all. No way to assess validity of claims. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Are sample sizes adequate for the statistical test used (e.g., parametric tests assume N > 30 or normality)?
- Are effect sizes reported alongside p-values?
- If multiple comparisons, are p-values corrected (Bonferroni, FDR)?
- For RCTs/experiments: are baseline characteristics reported (Table 1)?
- For null results: is power analysis mentioned (was N sufficient to detect meaningful effect)?
- Are results presented as point estimate ± CI, not just p < 0.05?

---

## Scoring Summary

**Publication-ready:** Scores ≥3 on all dimensions, OR ≤2 only on minor items (non-Major).  
**Minor revisions needed:** 1–2 dimensions score 2 (Major), rest ≥3. Author must address Majors.  
**Major revisions needed:** 3+ dimensions score 2 or more 1s. Substantial rework required.  
**Reject:** Multiple dimensions score 1, or core contribution is missing/invalid.

---

## Reviewer Guidance

When scoring, consider:
- **Best case:** Would this paper advance the field if everything is correct?
- **Plausibility:** Do findings make sense given the design?
- **Rigor:** Could another researcher reproduce this?
- **Honesty:** Does the author acknowledge uncertainty and limitations?
- **Positioning:** Does the paper make a clear contribution relative to prior work?

Remember: Perfection is not the bar. Competent execution, clear contribution, and honest limitations are the bar.
