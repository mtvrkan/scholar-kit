# Viva Voce Preparation & Defense

## 1. Committee Question Patterns by Chapter

### Chapter 1: Introduction & Background

**Pattern: Justify the problem and novelty.**

| Likely question | Your answer skeleton |
|---|---|
| "Why is this problem important?" | [Real-world impact OR theoretical gap]: "In [domain], [unsolved problem] affects [stakeholders/outcome]. Prior work [limitation], but..." |
| "What's new about your approach?" | "While prior work addresses [X], this thesis is the first to [your unique angle] under [specific constraint]." |
| "Who cares about this question?" | [Practitioners / researchers / policymakers]: Name 2–3 beneficiaries and one concrete use case. |
| "How does this differ from [similar thesis they might know]?" | "That work focused on [aspect A], while mine addresses [aspect B under new condition]." |
| "Is this a gap in English literature or a gap in Turkish literature?" | [Honest answer]: "The gap is [international / Turkey-specific / both]. I searched [databases] and found..." |

**Preparation:**
- Write your gap statement (1 paragraph, 3 sentences max).
- Prepare one concrete example of the impact (e.g., "If method X were deployed, practitioners would [reduce cost / improve outcome] by ~[%]").
- Anticipate: "But someone must have looked at this?" → Name 2 papers that came close, explain why they don't solve your RQ.

### Chapter 2: Literature Review

**Pattern: Demonstrate mastery of the field.**

| Likely question | Your answer skeleton |
|---|---|
| "Which paper is most central to your work?" | [Key foundational paper]: "X's work on [topic] established the baseline that I build on. [Key contribution]. Relevant because [mechanism/outcome]." |
| "How does your approach relate to the [competing] method Y?" | [Fair comparison]: "Method Y optimizes for [criterion A]; mine optimizes for [criterion B]. If you need [use case], Y is better. If you need [your use case], mine is better." |
| "What's the main limitation of the literature?" | [Honest synthesis]: "The field has studied [X component], but the combination of X + Y under [your specific condition] remains unexplored." |
| "Did you miss any major papers?" | [Confident]: "I searched [databases + keywords] exhaustively, updating through [date]. I'm confident in coverage of [subtopic], though new papers emerge constantly." |
| "Is there consensus on [key term]?" | [If yes/no]: "Consensus is [definition], but [author X] argues [alternative]. I use the consensus definition because..." |

**Preparation:**
- Develop a 2-minute summary of each major theme (3–4 themes typical).
- Create a one-page comparison table: paper × key contribution (bring a printout).
- Identify one "dark horse" paper that influenced your thinking — be ready to explain why it's not widely known.

### Chapter 3: Methodology

**Pattern: Defend every choice and anticipate "Why not...?"**

| Likely question | Your answer skeleton |
|---|---|
| "Why this design [experimental/observational/etc.] and not [alternative]?" | [Causal vs descriptive need, feasibility]: "My RQ requires [causal estimate / mapping], which [design] provides. [Alternative design] would require [resource/time] we don't have." |
| "How do you know your measurement is valid?" | [Reliability source]: "Instrument X has been validated by [prior study]; I used [Cronbach's α / test-retest / other]. Additionally, I [triangulated / checked / piloted]." |
| "Sample size N=X — is that enough?" | [Power or saturation]: "For [statistical test / qualitative saturation], N=X gives [power 0.8 / theme saturation at iteration Y]. Benchmarked against [comparable study]." |
| "What if [confound / threat] affects your results?" | [Honest limitation]: "This is a real threat. I addressed it by [design choice / statistical control]. Remaining risk: [scenario]. In [context], this matters because..." |
| "Why didn't you use [technology / method] X?" | [Resource, timing, or conceptual reason]: "I considered it. However, [cost / availability / conceptual mismatch]. For this RQ, [chosen method] is more appropriate." |

**Preparation:**
- Create a one-slide summary: design name → RQ alignment → sample/data → analysis → validity checks.
- List 3–5 threats to validity and have a 1-sentence mitigation for each.
- Bring a table of your hyperparameters (if ML) or measurement protocols (if empirical).
- Practice: "If you had more time/money, what would you change?" → Answer honestly but show you've thought it through.

### Chapter 4: Results

**Pattern: Explain findings clearly and humbly.**

| Likely question | Your answer skeleton |
|---|---|
| "Walk us through [specific result]." | [Narrative of the table/figure]: "Table 3 shows [what it compares]. The finding is: Method A (M=X, SD=Y) outperformed Method B (M=Z, SD=W) by Z%. Statistical test [t-test, etc.] gave p=0.05, suggesting [practical meaning]." |
| "What surprised you?" | [Genuine reflection]: "I expected [prediction], but [actual result]. The likely explanation is [mechanism]." |
| "Do these results generalize?" | [Honest scope]: "Within this sample/context: yes, reliably. Beyond this: unclear. [Threat to generalization]. Future work should test [population/context]." |
| "Why is [metric] important?" | [Justification]: "In this domain, [metric] reflects [real outcome]. A ±X% change in [metric] translates to [practical consequence]." |
| "[Competitor work] reported higher accuracy. How?" | [Fair comparison]: "Their study used [different design/data/metric]. On comparable benchmarks, results are [similar/explains difference]." |

**Preparation:**
- Create a "2-minute tour" of your key finding: problem → method → result → why it matters.
- Bring one sentence for each result that explains it to a layperson (non-specialist committee member).
- Prepare: "What's your confidence in this finding?" → Answer with your evidence (e.g., "High. N=5 runs, all converged; p<0.05 in 4/5; manually verified qualitative findings").

### Chapter 5: Discussion, Claims & Contributions

**Pattern: Show you've learned something and positioned it carefully.**

| Likely question | Your answer skeleton |
|---|---|
| "In one sentence: what's the main contribution?" | [Crisp claim]: "This thesis shows [finding/method] works under [condition], advancing the field by [mechanism/enabling new applications]." |
| "How does this compare to [competing approach]?" | [Fair trade-off]: "Approach A is faster; mine is more accurate. Use A if speed matters; use mine if precision is critical. A is simpler; mine requires [trade-off]." |
| "What are the real limitations?" | [Specific + honest]: "Generalizability is limited to [population/context]. Method requires [resource]. In future, [direction] would test boundaries." |
| "How do you respond to [contradictory finding from literature]?" | [Synthesis]: "That finding is about [context A]; mine is about [context B]. The difference is likely [mechanism]. Both are valid; context determines which applies." |
| "Who would use this in practice?" | [Concrete stakeholder + scenario]: "[Practitioner type] would use this to [specific task] in [scenario]. Barrier to adoption: [real barrier]. Mitigation: [path forward]." |
| "What's the next logical step?" | [Future work, specific]: "The natural extension is [clear next step] by [method change / population]. This would test [hypothesis] and inform [practical question]." |

**Preparation:**
- Write your thesis in 1 paragraph (50 words max): "This thesis investigates [RQ]. Using [method], I found [finding]. This matters because [implication]. Key limitation: [scope]."
- Anticipate the weakest link in your argument. Have a 2-sentence explanation ready.
- Prepare 2–3 "future work" ideas and rank them by feasibility (1 = you could start next month, 3 = someone else's PhD).

## 2. Answer Framework Templates

### For "But..." or "Why not...?" questions:
```
"That's a fair point. [Restate their concern accurately].
[Explanation: design choice / resource constraint / methodological reason].
[Evidence/authority]: [Prior work / discipline norm / your pilot].
[Honest limitation, if any]: In future, [direction to address this].
[Reframe positively]: Despite this, the thesis successfully [what you did achieve]."
```

### For questions about conflicting findings:
```
"Both findings are valid, but in different contexts.
Study A [condition/population]. Study B [condition/population].
My work [specifies which condition], which is important because [use case].
The field would benefit from [mapping condition 1 vs 2], which isn't done yet."
```

### For "Why not a different method?" questions:
```
"I considered [method]. Here's why [your choice] is better for this RQ:
[Criterion 1]: Mine is [advantage], theirs is [trade-off].
[Criterion 2]: [same].
Given this RQ [restate it], [your method] aligns better."
```

## 3. Presentation Outline (12–15 slides)

**Total time:** 20 minutes presentation + 10 minutes questions (common format). Slides are visual anchors, not reading material.

| Slide | Content | Speaking time |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Thesis title, student name, advisor, institution, date | 15 sec |
| Motivation | Problem in real-world terms (1 image or diagram), why it matters (1 sentence) | 1 min |
| RQ Slide | "This thesis asks: [RQ1], [RQ2], [RQ3]." | 30 sec |
| Background | 1–2 prior approaches (diagrams), gap they leave | 1 min |
| Your Approach | Method overview (flowchart or schematic), key innovations highlighted | 1.5 min |
| Methodology Detail | Study design, sample/data, analysis steps (text minimal, visuals maximum) | 1.5 min |
| Results Slide 1 | Main results (table or figure), one key finding highlighted | 1 min |
| Results Slide 2 | Comparison table or secondary results (e.g., ablation) | 1 min |
| Interpretation | "What this means": [RQ1] → [answer + implication]; repeat for RQ2, RQ3 | 1.5 min |
| Comparison | How yours differs from [key competitor] (use Venn diagram or table) | 1 min |
| Limitations | 2–3 key limitations (specific, not vague) | 45 sec |
| Implications | "If you remember one thing..." (1 sentence) + practical use case | 1 min |
| Future Work | 1–2 natural extensions, ranked by feasibility | 45 sec |
| Conclusion | Recap main claim (1 sentence) + closing thought | 30 sec |
| Thank You / Questions | Backup slides (extras, raw data, code snippets) follow if needed | — |

**Slide design rules:**
- Max 5 lines of text per slide.
- Figures/tables occupy ≥60% of slide space.
- Use one consistent color scheme (avoid rainbow).
- Large fonts: 28 pt min for body, 36 pt for headings.
- One key finding per results slide (don't cram).

## 4. Technical Troubleshooting During Defense

### If you blank on an answer:
```
"That's a great question. Let me think... [pause 3 sec].
My instinct is [direction]. The specific evidence is [statistic/quote from your thesis].
Does that address your question, or should I elaborate?"
```

### If they point out a flaw:
```
"You're right. [Acknowledge clearly].
[Explain your reasoning at the time OR explain why you misjudged].
Looking back, [what you'd do differently OR why it doesn't affect the main claim].
This is exactly the kind of thing [follow-up work / collaborators] would investigate."
```

### If the question is outside your scope:
```
"That's a really interesting question about [topic].
My thesis focuses on [scope], so I didn't explore [their question].
But based on [related work], I'd hypothesize [educated guess].
Definitely something for future work."
```

## 5. Post-Defense Scenarios

### If they ask for revisions:
- "Minor revisions" (1–2 weeks): typos, clarifications, minor reframing. You're done.
- "Major revisions" (1–2 months): redo analysis, expand sections, new experiments. Resubmit to advisor.
- Ensure you document all changes in a revision letter (point-by-point responses).

### Passing criteria vary by country/institution:
- **Turkish MSc**: Pass / Conditional pass (with revisions) / Fail (rare).
- **Turkish PhD**: Pass / Conditional pass / Return for major revision / Fail (very rare).

Know your institution's rubric in advance (ask your advisor).
