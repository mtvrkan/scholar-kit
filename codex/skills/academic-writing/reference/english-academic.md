# English Academic Writing: Turkish Authors' Common Issues

## Article Usage (The #1 Error)

Turkish has no articles (a/an/the). English requires them. This is the single most common error in Turkish-authored papers.

### Decision Tree for "The" vs. "A" vs. Ø (no article)

```
Is the noun countable (singular)?
  YES → Does it refer to a SPECIFIC thing (previously mentioned or unique)?
    YES → USE "the"
    NO  → USE "a" / "an"
  NO (plural or uncountable) → Is it SPECIFIC?
    YES → USE "the"
    NO  → USE NO ARTICLE (Ø)
```

### Examples (Turkish Authors' Typical Errors)

| Wrong | Correct | Explanation |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| "The research shows the results." | "The research shows **the results**." or "**Our** research shows **the results**." | "The" is correct here (specific: the results of this research) |
| "Deep learning is **a** powerful technique." | "Deep learning is **a** powerful technique." | First mention, specific example: use "a" |
| "**Ø Deep learning** is powerful." | "**Deep learning** is powerful." | General statement (uncountable noun = no article) ✓ |
| "We used **the model**." | "We used **a model**." (first mention) or "We used **the model**." (if previously mentioned) | First mention: "a"; later: "the" |
| "Results show **the accuracy** increased." | "Results show **that accuracy** increased." or "**The accuracy**…" (if specific to our method) | "Accuracy" is uncountable; only "the" if it refers to a specific accuracy (e.g., "the accuracy of our method") |

### The vs. A: Quick Rules

| Situation | Example | Article |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **General fact / first mention** | "We developed **a** classification method." | a / an |
| **Specific / already mentioned** | "The method achieved 95% accuracy. **The performance** exceeded expectations." | the |
| **Unique in context** | "**The** dataset contained 10,000 images." (because there's only one dataset in this study) | the |
| **Generic plural** | "Neural networks are powerful tools." | Ø (no article) |
| **Uncountable** | "Data **were** analyzed. **The analysis** took 2 hours." | Ø (data); the (specific analysis) |

### Common Turkish-Author Errors

| Error | Why It Happens | Fix |
|-------|---|---|
| "We used **the** MNIST dataset." | Turkish: "MNIST veri seti**ni** (accusative)"; feels like "the" | "We used **the** MNIST dataset." ✓ (correct—it's specific/unique) |
| "**The** machine learning models are powerful." | No articles in Turkish; every noun feels like it needs "the" | "Machine learning models are powerful." (general) or "**The** model we proposed is powerful." (specific) |
| "Results show **Ø** improvement." | Works in Turkish; wrong in English | "Results show **an** improvement." or "Results show improvement." (if uncountable context) |
| "We trained **the** network for 100 epochs." | Turkish: "ağı" (the network, accusative); feels singular/definite | "We trained **a** network for 100 epochs." (first mention) or "**The** network was trained…" (passive, specific) |

## Collocation Bank: Academic Verbs

A **collocation** is a pair of words that commonly go together. Using the wrong verb with a noun feels awkward to native speakers.

### What NOT to Do (Common Turkish-Author Errors)

| Wrong | Right | English |
|-------|-------|---------|
| "**have** a problem" | "**face** a challenge" or "**encounter** a problem" | encounter / address a problem |
| "**do** research" | "**conduct** research" | conduct research |
| "**make** a finding" | "**make** a finding" ✓ OR "**arrive at** a finding" | arrive at a finding |
| "**show** improvement" | "**demonstrate** improvement" | demonstrate improvement |
| "**use** a method" | "**employ** a method" or "**apply** a method" | employ a method |
| "**get** results" | "**obtain** results" or "**achieve** results" | obtain results |
| "**give** data" | "**provide** data" or "**collect** data" | provide data |

### Strong Academic Collocations (Use These)

| Verb + Object | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **conduct** research/study/experiment | "We **conducted** a study on 50 participants." | carry out |
| **demonstrate** improvement/effectiveness/findings | "Results **demonstrate** a 10% accuracy gain." | show conclusively |
| **establish** relationship/theory/framework | "Prior work **established** the link between X and Y." | prove, set up |
| **employ** method/technique/strategy | "We **employed** a case study approach." | use strategically |
| **obtain** results/data/findings | "We **obtained** high-quality data from…" | acquire, get |
| **propose** method/solution/framework | "We **propose** a novel loss function." | put forward |
| **provide** evidence/data/insight | "Our findings **provide** new insight into…" | give, offer |
| **reveal** pattern/trend/relationship | "Statistical analysis **revealed** a significant correlation." | uncover, show |
| **suggest** relationship/mechanism/implication | "Results **suggest** that X influences Y." | indicate |
| **yield** results/finding/outcome | "Experiments **yielded** 94% accuracy." | produce, result in |

### Verb + Preposition (Common Errors)

| Wrong | Correct | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| "focus in X" | "focus **on** X" | "We **focus on** the Methods section." |
| "compare to/with X" | "compare **to** (similarity)" / "**with** (difference)" | "Compared **with** prior work, our method…" (showing contrast) |
| "result to X" | "result **in** X" | "Overfitting **results in** poor generalization." |
| "contribute to/for X" | "contribute **to** X" | "This work **contributes to** the field." |
| "consist in X" | "consist **of** X" | "The dataset **consists of** 1000 images." |

## Nominalization Overload: Repair Patterns

**Nominalization** = turning verbs into nouns (e.g., "improve" → "improvement"). Too many nominalizations make writing dense and hard to parse.

### Spotting Nominalizations

| Noun Form | Original Verb | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| improvement | improve | "**Improvement** of accuracy was achieved." |
| implementation | implement | "**Implementation** of the algorithm…" |
| determination | determine | "**Determination** of hyperparameters…" |
| analysis | analyze | "**Analysis** of data reveals…" |

### Repair Pattern (Nominalization → Verb)

| Dense (Nominalized) | Clear (Verbified) |
|---|---|
| "**The implementation of the method** requires **understanding of the theory**." | "To **implement** the method, researchers must **understand** the underlying theory." |
| "**Improvement in accuracy through regularization** was **observed**." | "Regularization **improved** accuracy." |
| "**The training of the model** resulted in **achievement of 95% accuracy**." | "Training the model **achieved** 95% accuracy." |
| "**Consideration of the limitations** is **important** for **interpretation of the findings**." | "To **interpret** the findings, we must **consider** the limitations." |

### Rule of Thumb

- 1–2 nominalizations per sentence = OK.
- 3+ nominalizations = Too dense. Rewrite.
- Chain of nominalizations (noun + of + noun + of + noun) = Always refactor.

## Hedge Verbs & Phrases (Signaling Uncertainty)

Academic writing requires hedging (showing uncertainty). These are the right phrases:

### Hedge Verbs (Claim Strength)

| Strength | Verb | Example |
|----------|------|---------|
| **Strong** | demonstrate, establish, prove, show | "Results **demonstrate** that X causes Y." |
| **Moderate-strong** | indicate, suggest, provide evidence | "Findings **indicate** that X affects Y." |
| **Moderate** | suggest, appear, imply | "Results **appear** to support X." |
| **Weak** | may suggest, might, could, tend to | "One **might** argue that X influences Y." |

### Hedge Phrases

| Phrase | Strength | Example |
|--------|----------|---------|
| "It is possible that…" | Weak | "It is possible that future research will clarify this." |
| "Evidence suggests that…" | Moderate | "Evidence suggests that X is important." |
| "Our findings indicate…" | Moderate | "Our findings indicate a correlation." |
| "To our knowledge,…" | Weak-moderate | "To our knowledge, this is the first study combining X and Y." |
| "Arguably,…" | Moderate | "Arguably, the method is more efficient than prior work." |
