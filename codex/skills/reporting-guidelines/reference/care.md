# CARE: Case Reports

## Checklist (13 Items)

| # | Item | Location |
|----|------|----------|
| **1** | Title includes "case report"; identifies unique feature | Title |
| **2** | Keywords: 3–5 terms for indexing | Title page |
| **3** | Case demographics: age, gender, ethnicity, occupation, social background | Case Presentation |
| **4** | Case history: past medical/surgical history, medications, allergies, social/family history | Case Presentation |
| **5** | Timeline: chronological sequence of clinical events, investigations, interventions | Case Presentation |
| **6** | Presenting complaint + vital signs, physical findings, relevant negatives | Case Presentation |
| **7** | Diagnostic tests ordered, results (with reference ranges), imaging findings | Case Presentation |
| **8** | Diagnostic reasoning: differential, what led to diagnosis, what was ruled out | Case Presentation or Discussion |
| **9** | Intervention: drug/procedure names, doses, routes, duration, any adverse effects | Case Presentation |
| **10** | Follow-up and outcomes: clinical resolution, current status, time from intervention | Case Presentation or Results |
| **11** | Informed consent: written consent obtained (date) OR explanation if not obtainable | Methods or Results |
| **12** | Discussion: what is unique/instructive about case; positioning vs. literature; learning points | Discussion |
| **13** | Limitations: limitations of case (rare, single instance, recall bias, missing data) | Discussion |

## Timeline Requirement (Critical — Item 5)

**How to present:**

Option 1 (Narrative with dates):
> "Patient presented to ED on March 15, 2023, with chest pain. ECG on March 16 showed ST elevation. Troponin on March 17 was elevated at 0.08 μg/L (ref: <0.04). By March 20, troponin normalized and patient was discharged."

Option 2 (Table):
| Date | Event | Finding | Intervention |
|------|-------|---------|--------------|
| Mar 15 | ED presentation | Chest pain; BP 142/88 | History taken |
| Mar 16 | Admission | ECG: ST elevation | Started monitoring |
| Mar 17 | Ward | Troponin: 0.08 (high) | Aspirin ordered |
| Mar 20 | Discharge | Troponin normalized | Discharge with follow-up |

**Must include:** When symptoms started, when first medical contact, all tests with dates, when treatment started, when resolved.

## Consent Statement (Critical — Item 11)

**Examples:**

✓ "Written informed consent was obtained from the patient on December 15, 2023, for publication of this case report."

✓ "The patient passed away on [date]; written consent for publication was obtained from next of kin on [date]."

✓ "Attempts to locate the patient were unsuccessful ([methods used], [dates]). The case has been de-identified (see below) to ensure privacy."

**Must include:** Date consent obtained (or why unobtainable).

## De-identification (Item 3, 11)

**Remove/mask:**
- Patient name, initials, hospital ID, medical record number
- Specific dates (use "Day 1", "Day 2", or month/year only) if identifiable
- Specific locations (if identifiable; e.g., "a large teaching hospital")

**Retain:**
- Age, gender, clinical features necessary for the case
- Disease specifics

**Example:**
> "A 67-year-old female with type 2 diabetes presented to a tertiary care center in early 2023 with complaints of…"

NOT:

> "Ms. [Patient Name], age 67, female, presented to [Specific Hospital Name] on March 15, 2023, with…"

## Common Omissions

| Item | Missing? | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Timeline | Events in unclear order | Add dates or use "Day 1, Day 2…" table format |
| Consent statement | No mention of consent | State: "Informed consent was obtained on [date]" or explain why not obtainable |
| De-identification | Names/specific details included | Remove: patient name, specific institution, exact dates (use ranges) |
| Diagnostic reasoning | "Patient had X, so we diagnosed Y" (unclear why) | Explain: "We suspected Y because X could indicate Y or Z; we ruled out Z by testing [how]; diagnosis confirmed by [test result]" |
| Limitations | Not discussed | State: single case (not generalizable), recall bias if history from patient memory, missing certain tests if unavailable |
| Positioning vs literature | No reference to prior similar cases | State: "While X has been reported, this is the first case of X combined with Y in [population]" |

## Why Case Reports Matter

- Alert clinicians to rare presentations or complications
- Describe novel procedures or treatments
- Identify side effects or adverse events
- Challenge or support existing knowledge
- Generate hypotheses for future research
