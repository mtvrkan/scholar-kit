# Rubric: Qualitative Research Studies

---

## Dimension 1: Research Question Clarity & Rationale (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the research question clear? Why is qualitative the right approach?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Research question is explicit and focused (not: "What is X?" but "How do X experience Y? What factors influence Z?"). Rationale explains why qualitative is appropriate (exploring meanings, lived experience, process, etc.). Theoretical framework or lens is stated. |
| **4** | Research question is clear. Rationale for qualitative is present. Framework may be implicit. |
| **3** | Research question is present but somewhat vague. Rationale for qualitative is weak or missing. Framework unclear. |
| **2** | Research question is vague or implied. Rationale for qualitative approach is absent or poor. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No clear research question. Unjustified use of qualitative method. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Can you state the RQ in one sentence?
- Does the RQ call for qualitative exploration (meanings, process, context) or could it be answered quantitatively?
- Is a guiding framework present (phenomenology, grounded theory, etc.)?

---

## Dimension 2: Researcher Positionality & Reflexivity (1–5)

**Definition:** Are researchers transparent about their background and potential biases?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Clear statement of researcher background (credentials, experience, relationship to topic). Explicit acknowledgment of potential biases or assumptions (e.g., "We came to this research believing X; data surprised us by revealing Y"). Evidence of reflexivity (e.g., research team discussed interpretations, bracketed assumptions). |
| **4** | Researcher background stated. Biases or assumptions mentioned. Some reflexivity evident. |
| **3** | Researcher background present but minimal. Reflexivity not explicitly discussed. |
| **2** | Researcher background missing or vague. No acknowledgment of potential bias. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No information on researcher(s). Interpretation presented as objective without reflexivity. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Do you know who conducted the research and what they bring to the analysis?
- Is there evidence the researchers questioned their own interpretations?
- Did the research team debrief or discuss divergent interpretations?

---

## Dimension 3: Sampling & Participant Recruitment (1–5)

**Definition:** Is sampling transparent? Are participants adequately described?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Sampling strategy explicit (purposive, convenience, snowball, etc.). Inclusion/exclusion criteria stated. Recruitment procedure described (how were participants found, approached, consented?). Participant demographics clearly presented (table or narrative: N, age, gender, occupation, other relevant characteristics). Saturation mentioned and justified (when/why sampling stopped). |
| **4** | Sampling strategy and criteria stated. Recruitment mostly described. Demographics presented. Saturation mentioned. |
| **3** | Sampling strategy present but vague. Demographics incomplete. Saturation not discussed. |
| **2** | Sampling strategy unclear. Participant characteristics poorly described. Saturation not addressed. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No information on sampling or participants. N unknown. Recruitment procedure opaque. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- How many participants? Is N justified (saturation reached, or other rationale)?
- Are inclusion/exclusion criteria clear so readers can assess applicability?
- Are demographics reported in a way that helps readers assess generalizability (e.g., "mostly university-educated, urban, middle-class")?

---

## Dimension 4: Data Collection Methods & Procedures (1–5)

**Definition:** How were data collected? Are procedures detailed and transparent?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Data collection method(s) specified (interviews, focus groups, observation, documents, etc.). Interview/FG topic guide provided or well-described. Setting and duration noted (e.g., "60–90 min semi-structured interviews in quiet room"). Audio-recorded? Transcribed verbatim? By whom? Data management procedures described (how were files stored, pseudonymized, secured?). |
| **4** | Data collection method clear. Topic guide mentioned. Setting/duration mostly specified. Transcription procedure mentioned. Data security addressed. |
| **3** | Data collection method stated. Topic guide not shown. Duration unclear. Transcription procedure vague. |
| **2** | Data collection method vague. No topic guide. Setting/duration missing. Transcription not described. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Data collection procedure opaque. No information on how data were gathered or managed. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Could another researcher conduct similar interviews/FGs using the topic guide?
- Were interviews audio-recorded (best practice for accuracy)?
- Were transcripts verbatim (including pauses, filler words) or cleaned?
- How were participant confidentiality and data security managed?

---

## Dimension 5: Data Analysis Procedure (1–5)

**Definition:** How were data analyzed? Is the process transparent and rigorous?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Analysis method named explicitly (thematic analysis, grounded theory, phenomenological analysis, etc.). Coding approach described (deductive, inductive, hybrid). Multi-coder analysis: ≥2 independent coders; inter-coder agreement reported (κ, % agreement). Codebook or coding examples provided. Analysis software named (NVivo, Atlas.ti) or manual method described. Iterative refinement documented (codes refined as new data reviewed). |
| **4** | Analysis method stated. Coding approach described. Multi-coder analysis mostly reported. Analysis tool mentioned. |
| **3** | Analysis method present but vague. Single coder or agreement not reported. No coding examples. |
| **2** | Analysis method unclear. Single coder without justification. No transparent process. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No analysis procedure described. How themes were identified is opaque. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Would another researcher, following this procedure, arrive at similar themes?
- Were coders trained? Were they blind to participants' identities/outcomes?
- How were disagreements resolved (discussion to consensus, third arbiter)?
- Is there a sample of coded data (e.g., "Example: Interview 5, line 23–31, coded as 'powerlessness'")?

---

## Dimension 6: Theme–Quote–Source Chain (CRITICAL) (1–5)

**Definition:** Are findings grounded in participant data? Is every quote attributed and sourced?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Every quote is verbatim, attributed to participant ID (P01, P02, etc. or pseudonym), and page-numbered or timestamped. Each theme has ≥3 strong supporting quotes from different participants. Context provided before each quote (who is this person, why does this quote matter?). Divergent or conflicting views discussed. No orphan claims (claims without evidence). |
| **4** | Most quotes attributed and sourced. Most themes have multiple supporting quotes. Mostly page-numbered. Divergent views mostly addressed. |
| **3** | Quotes attributed but sometimes without page numbers. Some themes have only 1–2 quotes. Some context present. Divergent views mentioned but not deeply explored. |
| **2** | Quotes missing page numbers. Themes not uniformly supported by multiple quotes. Orphan claims present (interpreted findings without quotes). **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Quotes not attributed or sourced. Findings presented without evidence. Claims appear to be researcher impositions. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Can you trace each major theme back to specific participants and quotes?
- Are page/timestamp references provided for verification?
- If a theme is stated, are there at least 2–3 illustrative quotes?
- Are minority/conflicting views represented (not just consensus)?

---

## Dimension 7: Saturation & Sufficiency (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the sample size justified? When/why did data collection stop?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Saturation explicitly discussed: "Data collection continued until saturation was reached at N=X" (with evidence, e.g., "by interview 18–20, no new themes emerged"). Rationale for stopping is clear. Sample size is defensible for the question (depth over N). |
| **4** | Saturation mentioned. Sample size seems adequate. Justification present. |
| **3** | Saturation mentioned but not deeply discussed. Sample size adequate but justification unclear. |
| **2** | Saturation not discussed. Sample size seems arbitrary or too small without justification. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No mention of saturation or stopping criteria. Sample size unjustified (N=3 with no explanation). **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Did the authors conduct enough interviews/FGs to reach saturation (usually N=12–30 for interviews)?
- Is stopping criterion explicit (saturation, resource limit, other)?
- For small N (e.g., N=5), is there a compelling reason (e.g., case study, intensive interviews)?

---

## Dimension 8: Trustworthiness Strategies (1–5)

**Definition:** What steps were taken to ensure credibility, dependability, and authenticity?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | ≥2 trustworthiness strategies employed from: member checking (participants reviewed findings; did they agree?), peer debriefing (research team discussed interpretations), triangulation (multiple data sources or methods), negative case analysis (cases that don't fit the theme are examined), audit trail (analysis decisions documented), thick description (detailed context for transferability). Results of strategies reported (e.g., "Member checking: 4 of 5 participants confirmed themes; 1 suggested revision to theme X"). |
| **4** | 2–3 trustworthiness strategies mentioned. Mostly reported how they were applied. |
| **3** | 1–2 strategies mentioned but application vague. Results of strategies not clearly reported. |
| **2** | Trustworthiness not addressed or only superficially mentioned. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No attention to trustworthiness; interpretation presented as unquestionably valid. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Did participants review findings (member checking)?
- Did the research team discuss and debate interpretations (peer debriefing)?
- Were competing interpretations considered (negative case analysis)?
- How do findings hold up under scrutiny? (What would falsify the theme?)

---

## Dimension 9: Limitations of Scope & Generalizability (1–5)

**Definition:** Are limitations acknowledged? Is generalizability claim appropriate?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Limitations clearly stated: sample characteristics (homogeneity limits generalizability), setting (single location, specific context), timing (point-in-time snapshot), and researcher perspective (positionality may influence interpretation). Generalizability framed as "transferability" (whether findings apply to similar contexts, not statistical generalizability). |
| **4** | Limitations discussed. Generalizability framed appropriately as transferability. Some limitation areas covered. |
| **3** | Limitations mentioned but brief. Generalizability discussed but somewhat confused (mixing statistical and qualitative framing). |
| **2** | Limitations vague or minimized. Generalizability overstated (treated as if findings are universal). **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No limitations section. Findings presented as universally applicable. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Are demographic limits acknowledged (e.g., "mostly female, educated, urban; may not apply to men, less educated, rural populations")?
- Does the paper acknowledge that themes emerged in a specific context and time?
- Is generalizability claimed modestly (e.g., "likely applicable to similar settings" vs. "definitive truth")?

---

## Dimension 10: Abstract ↔ Body Consistency (1–5)

**Definition:** Do abstract findings match body findings?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Abstract accurately summarizes research question, method, main themes, and implications. Quotes or specific findings match body. No unsupported claims. Tone matches (qualitative language like "participants described," not overconfident "proved"). |
| **4** | Abstract mostly accurate. Minor discrepancies. Themes in abstract match body. |
| **3** | Abstract has some unsupported statements. Themes somewhat vague. Reader needs full paper to verify abstract. |
| **2** | Abstract makes claims not well-supported in body. Overstates certainty. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Abstract contradicts body findings. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Does the abstract list themes that appear in body with supporting quotes?
- Does abstract hedging language match body? (Qualitative studies use "described," "identified," not "proved")

---

## Scoring Summary

**Publication-ready:** All dimensions ≥3; no Major issues.  
**Minor revisions:** 1–2 Major issues (e.g., saturation not discussed, limited trustworthiness strategies). Author addresses in 1 round.  
**Major revisions:** 3+ Major issues, especially Dimensions 6 (quote-source chain) or 7 (saturation). Substantial rework needed.  
**Reject:** Findings appear to lack grounding in data; themes imposed by researcher; credibility severely compromised.

---

## Reviewer Guidance

- **Qualitative rigor ≠ quantitative rigor.** It's about depth, transparency, and trustworthiness, not statistical power.
- **Red flag:** Findings presented without participant quotes or context (researcher interpretation without evidence).
- **Red flag:** Saturation not mentioned (unclear if N=5 was "enough").
- **Strength:** Multiple quotes per theme, triangulation method, participant member-checking, documented reflexivity.
