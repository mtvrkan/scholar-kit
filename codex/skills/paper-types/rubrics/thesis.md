# Rubric: Thesis (BSc/MSc/PhD)

---

## Dimension 1: Research Question & Contribution (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the research question clear? Is the contribution appropriate for the level?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Research question is clear, focused, and stated early. Contribution is explicitly stated and appropriate for the degree level: BSc shows competence in method/analysis; MSc demonstrates mastery + independent work; PhD shows novel advancement to the field. Contribution is justified relative to literature. |
| **4** | RQ is clear. Contribution is stated and mostly appropriate for level. Justification present. |
| **3** | RQ is present but could be clearer. Contribution is somewhat vague or borderline for level. Justification weak. |
| **2** | RQ is vague or implicit. Contribution is unclear or under-scoped for the degree level. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No clear RQ. Contribution is missing or trivial for the level. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee):**
- **BSc:** Does the student demonstrate mastery of the method? Can they execute a competent empirical or theoretical project?
- **MSc:** Is the contribution novel (not just replicating prior work)? Is it original in data/method/analysis?
- **PhD:** Does the contribution advance the field? Is it publishable? Does it open new research directions?

---

## Dimension 2: Literature Review & Positioning (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the literature comprehensively covered? Is the contribution positioned within it?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Literature review is thorough (20–50+ sources depending on field). Organized thematically, not chronologically. Major theories, key papers, and recent work covered. Review explains the state of knowledge, identifies gaps, and justifies why this RQ is important. Contribution is explicitly positioned ("This fills the gap by…"). |
| **4** | Literature review is solid and mostly organized thematically. Most key papers included. Gaps identified. Contribution positioned. |
| **3** | Literature review present but incomplete or weak organization. Some key gaps in coverage. Positioning somewhat vague. |
| **2** | Literature review is thin, incomplete, or mostly chronological list. Gaps not identified. Contribution positioning unclear. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Inadequate literature review. Key papers missing. Positioning absent. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee):**
- Would an expert in this area recognize all key prior work?
- Is the gap (what's not known) clearly articulated?
- Does the thesis explicitly say how it addresses that gap?

---

## Dimension 3: Research Design & Methodology (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the method appropriate and well-justified? Are procedures detailed?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Research design is well-justified (e.g., "Case study because we need in-depth understanding of X"; "Experiment to test causality of Y"). Method section is detailed (replicable). All procedures are explained: sampling, data collection, analysis. Validity threats or limitations are acknowledged. Ethical approval (if needed) is documented. |
| **4** | Design is appropriate and justified. Method mostly detailed. Procedures mostly clear. Ethics mostly addressed. |
| **3** | Design is reasonable but justification could be stronger. Method section has some gaps. Procedures somewhat unclear. Ethics barely mentioned. |
| **2** | Design choice is not well-justified. Method section vague; replicability uncertain. Ethical issues not addressed. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Method is inappropriate or opaque. Severe ethical issues. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee):**
- Could another researcher replicate this study from the Methods chapter?
- Is the sample size or scope justified (power analysis, saturation, etc.)?
- Are ethical principles respected (IRB approval, informed consent, privacy)?
- Are limitations of the design acknowledged?

---

## Dimension 4: Results Presentation & Coherence (1–5)

**Definition:** Are findings clearly presented? Do they answer the RQ?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Results section presents findings organized by RQ (each RQ has a subsection). Results directly address RQs/hypotheses stated in Introduction. Figures and tables are informative and self-contained. Results include appropriate summary stats, measures of variability, or thematic organization. Sufficient detail for reader to understand patterns. |
| **4** | Results mostly address RQs. Mostly well-organized. Figures/tables mostly clear. Detail adequate. |
| **3** | Results address RQs but organization could improve. Some figures/tables unclear. Detail sometimes sparse. |
| **2** | Results loosely connected to RQs or disorganized. Figures/tables missing or unclear. Insufficient detail. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Results don't clearly address RQs. Presentation is opaque. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee):**
- Can you trace each RQ from Introduction → Results → Discussion?
- Is every table/figure necessary and well-captioned?
- Are results presented with adequate granularity (not just summaries)?

---

## Dimension 5: Discussion & Interpretation (1–5)

**Definition:** Are findings interpreted thoughtfully? Are implications discussed?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Discussion interprets results in light of literature. Explains why findings are significant (confirm prior work, extend it, contradict it). Addresses implications for theory and practice. Limitations are honestly discussed (not hidden). Alternative explanations are considered. Future research is suggested. |
| **4** | Discussion interprets findings well. Most implications discussed. Limitations mentioned. Future research suggested. |
| **3** | Discussion interprets findings but could go deeper. Implications somewhat vague. Limitations briefly mentioned. |
| **2** | Discussion is superficial or missing. Findings not interpreted relative to literature. Limitations minimized. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No meaningful discussion. Findings presented without interpretation. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee):**
- Does the student show understanding of what their findings mean?
- Are findings compared to prior work (aligned, contradictory, or novel)?
- Are limitations honestly assessed?
- Do practical/theoretical implications make sense?

---

## Dimension 6: Conclusions & Contributions (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the conclusion clear? Are contributions reiterated?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Conclusion section summarizes key findings and restates the contribution. Reiterates how this work advances the field. Acknowledges limitations. Suggests future directions. Does not overstate findings. Clear statement: "In summary, this thesis [contribution] by [method]." |
| **4** | Conclusion summarizes findings and contributions. Mostly clear. Limitations and future work mentioned. |
| **3** | Conclusion present but may be vague or overstate. Some limitations/future work discussed. |
| **2** | Conclusion is weak or missing. Contribution unclear. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No clear conclusion or conclusion is incoherent. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee):**
- If a reader skips to the conclusion, will they understand the main contribution?
- Are claims in the conclusion supported by results?

---

## Dimension 7: Writing Quality & Organization (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the thesis well-written and organized logically?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Thesis is well-organized with clear chapter progression. Writing is clear and professional (minimal grammar/spelling errors). Paragraphs are coherent; ideas flow logically. Technical terms are defined. Formatting is consistent (citations, headings, figure numbering). Length is appropriate. |
| **4** | Thesis is well-organized and mostly clear. Minor writing issues. Formatting mostly consistent. |
| **3** | Organization is adequate but could improve. Writing is clear but has occasional issues. Formatting somewhat inconsistent. |
| **2** | Organization is unclear; reader struggles to follow. Writing has multiple errors or is dense. Formatting is inconsistent. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Poorly organized and written. Difficult to read. Major formatting problems. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee):**
- Does the thesis follow a logical chapter structure (Introduction → Literature → Methods → Results → Discussion → Conclusion)?
- Are headings clear and hierarchical?
- Is there a table of contents? List of figures/tables?
- Are citations formatted consistently (institutional preference)?

---

## Dimension 8: Chapter Coherence & Cross-References (1–5)

**Definition:** Do chapters logically connect? Are cross-references clear?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Each chapter has clear purpose and contributes to overall thesis. RQs stated in Ch.1 ↔ addressed in Results (Ch.4) ↔ discussed in Ch.5 → reflected in Conclusion (Ch.6). Cross-references between chapters are frequent (e.g., "As discussed in Chapter 2…"). No chapter is orphaned (all contribute to main argument). |
| **4** | Chapters mostly cohere. RQ-Results-Discussion mapping mostly clear. Some cross-references. |
| **3** | Chapters connect but mapping could be clearer. Limited cross-references. One chapter feels tangential. |
| **2** | Chapter organization is loose. RQ-Results-Discussion mapping unclear. Minimal cross-references. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Chapters feel disconnected. No clear mapping of RQ through results/discussion. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee):**
- If you skip a chapter, does the thesis still make sense?
- Do headings within chapters flow logically?
- Does the Results chapter directly address the RQs from Chapter 1?

---

## Dimension 9: Figures, Tables & Appendices (1–5)

**Definition:** Are visuals appropriate? Are appendices well-used?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Figures and tables are informative and necessary (not filler). Captions are self-contained. List of Figures and Tables is provided. All figures are referenced in text. Appendices contain supplementary materials (detailed procedures, additional data, questionnaires) not essential to main narrative. Appendices are referenced. |
| **4** | Figures/tables mostly appropriate. Captions clear. Some appendices used. Lists of figures/tables provided. |
| **3** | Some unnecessary figures/tables. Captions adequate but could be clearer. Appendices present but sparse. |
| **2** | Figures/tables are poorly organized or captions are vague. Appendices missing or poorly organized. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Figures/tables are inadequate or missing. No appendices or they are disorganized. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee):**
- Can you understand each figure/table from its caption alone?
- Does every figure/table serve a purpose (not just decorative)?
- Are supplementary materials appropriately relegated to appendices?

---

## Dimension 10: Positioning for Publication/Impact (1–5)

**Definition:** (PhD & advanced MSc) Is the work publishable? Is it positioned for impact?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Work contains 1+ publishable paper (journal or conference). Contribution is novel and positioned for the target field. Citation of relevant literature suggests impact potential. Reproducibility is supported (methods detailed, code/data statements). Work suggests future research directions. |
| **4** | Work is mostly publishable with minor revisions. Contribution is solid. Potential impact is present. Reproducibility mostly supported. |
| **3** | Work has publication potential but would need revisions. Contribution is incremental. Impact potential is limited. |
| **2** | Work is unlikely to be published as-is. Contribution is narrow or unclear. Publication path not evident. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Work is not suitable for publication. Contribution is trivial or flawed. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee — PhD level especially):**
- Would this work be accepted at a top-tier venue in the field?
- Has the student thought about target journals/conferences?
- Could a reader reproduce the work based on the thesis?
- Does the work open new questions for future research?

---

## Dimension 11: Ethical Compliance & Integrity (1–5)

**Definition:** Are ethical standards met? Is the work original and properly attributed?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | IRB/ethics approval documented (if needed). All human subjects' protections in place (informed consent, privacy, security). Citations are accurate and complete (all sources cited; no plagiarism). Figures, data, and results are truthfully presented (no manipulation, fabrication, or misrepresentation). Author's contributions vs. advisor/collaborator contributions are clear (if co-authored work). |
| **4** | Ethics mostly compliant. Citations mostly accurate. Integrity mostly evident. |
| **3** | Ethics compliance unclear or partial. Citation accuracy mostly good. Integrity assumed but not explicit. |
| **2** | Ethics compliance questionable or missing. Citation issues present (some sources not cited). Integrity concerns. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Ethical violations (unethical research conduct, plagiarism, data fabrication) or severe citation issues. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks (Committee — all levels):**
- Is the IRB approval letter included or referenced?
- Are all significant quotes and ideas properly attributed?
- Are all figures original or properly licensed/cited?
- Does the thesis acknowledge data limitations honestly?

---

## Scoring Summary

**Pass / Accept:** Most dimensions ≥3; no Major issues.  
**Pass with Minor Revisions:** 1–2 Major issues; student can address in revision phase before submission.  
**Conditional / Major Revisions:** 3+ Major issues; substantial rework needed; defense deferred.  
**Fail / Reject:** Multiple Major issues, especially Dimensions 1–2 (RQ/contribution) or Dimension 11 (integrity), or systematic failure to meet academic standards.

---

## Committee Guidance

**Degree-level expectations:**
- **BSc:** Competent empirical or theoretical project. Mastery of method. Clear communication. Contribution is demonstrating the method works or providing analysis within a subfield.
- **MSc:** Original work (novel data, novel analysis, or novel synthesis). Advancement beyond prior coursework. Publishable or nearly publishable. Contribution should be recognizable as a step forward.
- **PhD:** Significant contribution to the field. Novel ideas, methods, or findings. Work that the field will cite and build on. Publishable in top venues. Demonstrates independent scholarship.

**Red flags across all levels:**
- Thin or missing literature review.
- Vague or missing contribution.
- Results that don't answer the RQ.
- Ethical issues (IRB missing, plagiarism, data integrity concerns).
- Poor writing that obscures ideas.

**Strengths:**
- Clear RQ with well-justified contribution.
- Thorough literature review positioning the work.
- Rigorous methods and honest limitations discussion.
- Results clearly addressing RQ.
- Thoughtful interpretation and implications.
- Professional presentation and integrity.
