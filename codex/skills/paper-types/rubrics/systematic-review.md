# Rubric: Systematic Review / Scoping Review / Narrative Review

---

## Dimension 1: Contribution Clarity & Novelty (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the review question clear? Is the scope justified? Is the synthesis novel/needed?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Review question is explicit (PICO/PEO clearly stated). Rationale for the review is compelling (gap in existing knowledge, summarizing emerging evidence, or reconciling contradictions). Scope is justified (e.g., "Why these databases? Why this year range?"). |
| **4** | Review question is clear. Rationale is solid. Scope is mostly justified. |
| **3** | Review question is stated but could be clearer. Rationale is present but weak. Scope justification is implicit. |
| **2** | Review question is vague or implicit. Rationale is missing or weak. Scope appears arbitrary. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No clear review question. No rationale provided. Scope is unjustified. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Can you state the review question in one sentence?
- Why is this question important? Is the gap real (not just "no one has reviewed this yet")?
- Is scope justified (databases, year range, language, design inclusion/exclusion)?

---

## Dimension 2: Search Strategy Reproducibility (1–5)

**Definition:** Can readers replicate the search? Is strategy comprehensive and documented?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Full search strategy documented: databases searched (with dates), exact query syntax per database, hits per database, any filters applied (language, design, etc.). Multiple databases (≥3). Search log provided or easy to verify. |
| **4** | Search strategy mostly documented. Minor gaps (e.g., one database's exact query not shown). Usually ≥3 databases. |
| **3** | Search strategy mentioned but vague (e.g., "searched PubMed for X"). Databases listed but queries not shown. Hand searches not mentioned. |
| **2** | Search strategy poorly documented. Queries incomplete or hidden. Single database only. Reproducibility uncertain. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No search strategy described or it is completely unreproducible. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Could another researcher re-run the exact same search?
- Were multiple databases used (not just one)?
- Were forward/backward citation tracking mentioned?
- Are hit counts reported (identified N → screened N)?
- Is search date documented (important for reproducibility)?

---

## Dimension 3: Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria – Explicit & Applied Consistently (1–5)

**Definition:** Are inclusion/exclusion criteria stated upfront? Are they applied consistently by reviewers?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria listed (population, intervention, outcome, study design, language, year). Criteria defined clearly so reviewers can apply them uniformly. ≥2 independent reviewers screened at all stages; inter-rater agreement reported. |
| **4** | Criteria stated and clear. Mostly ≥2 independent reviewers; agreement mostly reported. |
| **3** | Criteria stated but somewhat vague. Single reviewer screening for some stages, or inter-rater agreement not reported. |
| **2** | Criteria vague or incomplete. Single reviewer screening. No mention of inter-rater agreement. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Criteria missing or ad-hoc. Reviewer bias apparent. No transparency. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Are criteria concrete (e.g., "RCTs only" is clear; "good quality studies" is vague)?
- Were disagreements resolved? How (consensus discussion, third reviewer)?
- What was inter-rater agreement (κ)? (≥0.60 is acceptable; ≥0.75 is good)
- Could another reviewer apply these criteria the same way?

---

## Dimension 4: Study Selection & Flow Diagram (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the selection process transparent? Does PRISMA flow diagram accurately track numbers?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | PRISMA flow diagram present and complete: identified N → deduplicated N → screened titles N → excluded (with reasons) N → assessed full texts N → excluded (with reasons) N → included N. Numbers are internally consistent (e.g., screened = dedup + new hits). Exclusion reasons are detailed. |
| **4** | Flow diagram present and mostly complete. Numbers mostly consistent. Exclusion reasons brief but present. |
| **3** | Flow diagram present but incomplete (missing some numbers or reasons). Consistency uncertain. |
| **2** | Flow diagram missing or incomplete. Numbers don't add up or exclusion reasons are vague. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No flow diagram. Selection process is opaque. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Do the numbers in the flow diagram add up logically?
- Are exclusion reasons listed (can spot bias, e.g., "excluded because not in English" reveals language restriction)?
- Does final N (included studies) match references cited in Results?

---

## Dimension 5: Data Extraction & Characteristics (1–5)

**Definition:** Is data extraction documented? Are included studies characterized?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Data extraction form/template shown or described. Data extracted by ≥2 reviewers (or 1 + verification). Characteristics table of included studies (author, year, country, design, population, intervention, outcomes, funding) is comprehensive and well-organized. Table easily identifies patterns (e.g., sample sizes, study designs). |
| **4** | Data extraction documented. Characteristics table present and informative. |
| **3** | Data extraction described briefly. Characteristics table present but sparse (missing some columns or details). |
| **2** | Data extraction procedure vague. Characteristics table missing or incomplete. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No data extraction described. No characteristics table. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Is the characteristics table detailed enough to spot patterns and heterogeneity?
- Are funding sources of included studies reported (marker of conflict/bias)?
- Are design types clear (how many RCTs vs. observational, etc.)?

---

## Dimension 6: Synthesis & Analysis Rigor (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the synthesis beyond list-making? Are findings coherently organized?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Synthesis is thematic (organized by concept/finding, not chronologically). Meta-analysis if quantitative (with quality assessment, heterogeneity, subgroups). Narrative synthesis if qualitative (conceptual organization, explanatory models, themes extracted across studies). Conflicts/divergent findings explicitly discussed. |
| **4** | Synthesis is organized thematically/conceptually. Meta-analysis well-conducted or narrative synthesis coherent. Most conflicts discussed. |
| **3** | Synthesis present but may be partly chronological or list-like. Meta-analysis or narrative synthesis attempted but incomplete. Some conflicts addressed. |
| **2** | Synthesis is mostly a list of studies ("Smith et al. found X, Jones et al. found Y…"). Meta-analysis poorly conducted or absent when appropriate. Conflicts ignored. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No synthesis. Just studies listed. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Is each paragraph arguing a point with evidence from multiple studies, or is it just summarizing one study?
- If meta-analysis: is heterogeneity discussed? Subgroups explored? Sensitivity analysis done?
- Are conflicting findings acknowledged? How are they explained?
- Is quality of evidence assessed (GRADE, ROBIS, or similar)?

---

## Dimension 7: Risk of Bias Assessment (1–5)

**Definition:** (For systematic reviews only; scoping reviews don't assess bias) Are study qualities evaluated? Is bias assessment transparent?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Risk-of-bias tool specified (Cochrane ROB2, ROBINS-I, etc.). ≥2 independent assessors; agreement reported. ROB summary table (per study, per outcome). Concerns about publication bias or small-study effects addressed (funnel plot, Egger's test). |
| **4** | ROB tool used and applied to most studies. Agreement mostly reported. Bias summary present. Publication bias considered. |
| **3** | ROB tool used but applied inconsistently or to subset of studies. Agreement not reported. Publication bias not addressed. |
| **2** | ROB assessment superficial or missing. No mention of publication bias. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No risk-of-bias assessment. Systematic review claims certainty without examining bias. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Is the ROB tool appropriate for the study designs included?
- Were findings stratified by study quality (e.g., high-quality RCTs vs. low-quality observational)?
- Does the review acknowledge that a strong pooled effect might be driven by low-quality studies?

---

## Dimension 8: Certainty of Evidence Assessment (1–5)

**Definition:** Is evidence certainty rated (GRADE, CASP, etc.)? Are conclusions proportionate to certainty?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Evidence certainty rated using GRADE or equivalent (high, moderate, low, very low). Ratings justified per outcome (accounting for ROB, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias). Conclusions are proportionate (strong conclusions only for high certainty). |
| **4** | Certainty assessment attempted. Mostly justified. Conclusions mostly proportionate. |
| **3** | Certainty mentioned but not formalized. Ratings unclear or only for some outcomes. Conclusions sometimes overstate certainty. |
| **2** | Certainty not assessed or very superficial. Conclusions may overstate strength of evidence. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No certainty assessment. Conclusions presented as fact despite weak evidence. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Does the review acknowledge limitations that reduce certainty (small N, high heterogeneity, publication bias)?
- Are strong recommendations made only for high-certainty evidence?
- Is there a table of GRADE ratings per outcome?

---

## Dimension 9: Limitations & Scope Honesty (1–5)

**Definition:** Are limitations (search, selection, heterogeneity, etc.) acknowledged?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Comprehensive limitations section addressing: search limits (language, databases, unpublished literature), selection bias risk (≥2 reviewers, agreement reported), heterogeneity (I², reasons explored), publication bias, generalizability (populations, settings, interventions included). Limitations are realistic, not over-apologetic. |
| **4** | Limitations well-discussed. Most areas covered. Tone is balanced. |
| **3** | Limitations mentioned but brief or incomplete. Some areas not addressed. |
| **2** | Limitations vague or minimized. Major limitations not acknowledged. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No limitations section. Systematic review presented as definitive despite obvious limits. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Is language restriction (e.g., English only) acknowledged?
- Are unpublished/gray literature limitations mentioned?
- Does the review acknowledge heterogeneity and explore reasons?
- Are population/setting/intervention limits discussed (who does this apply to)?

---

## Dimension 10: PRISMA Compliance & Reporting Completeness (1–5)

**Definition:** Does the manuscript cover the PRISMA 2020 (or PRISMA-ScR for scoping) 27 items?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | ≥24 of 27 PRISMA items present and well-reported. Minor items (e.g., funding of review) clearly stated. Protocol/registration mentioned (PROSPERO or similar). Deviations from protocol explained. |
| **4** | 22–23 items clearly present. Most details adequate. Minor items may lack detail. |
| **3** | 20–21 items present. Some items superficial or missing. Checklist compliance partial. |
| **2** | <20 items clearly present. Multiple important items missing (e.g., no flow diagram, no funding statement). **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | <15 items or no attempt to address PRISMA. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Is PRISMA checklist provided (as supplementary material or in appendix)?
- Are all 27 items addressed somewhere in the paper?
- Is the protocol pre-registered? (PROSPERO ID for SRs; OSF for scoping)
- Are any deviations from protocol explained?

---

## Dimension 11: Abstract ↔ Body Consistency (1–5)

**Definition:** Do abstract claims match body findings?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Abstract accurately summarizes objectives, methods, findings, and conclusions. No unsupported claims. Numbers in abstract match body. Conclusion in abstract is supported by synthesis. |
| **4** | Abstract mostly accurate and consistent. Minor discrepancies. |
| **3** | Abstract has some unsupported or vague claims. Reader might need full paper to verify abstract. |
| **2** | Abstract makes claims not clearly supported in body. Overstates certainty or significance. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Abstract contradicts or is unrelated to body findings. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- If abstract claims "X is effective," does the body support this? With what certainty?
- Are numbers (N of studies, effect sizes) consistent between abstract and results?

---

## Scoring Summary (Systematic Review / Scoping Review)

**Publication-ready:** Most dimensions ≥3; no Major issues or only 1 minor Major issue.  
**Minor revisions:** 1–2 Major issues; author can address in 1 round.  
**Major revisions:** 3+ Major issues or fundamental flaws in synthesis/bias assessment.  
**Reject:** Multiple dimension scores of 1, or core flaws (search not reproducible, synthesis is just a list, PRISMA compliance severe).

---

## Reviewer Guidance

- **Systematic reviews** set a high bar: reproducible search, ≥2 reviewers at all stages, formal risk-of-bias assessment, quantitative synthesis with quality checks.
- **Scoping reviews** have lower bar: mapping the field, not systematic depth. Still need clear criteria, ≥2 reviewers for screening.
- **Narrative reviews** are legitimate but must acknowledge selectivity and synthesis (not annotated bibliography).
- An incomplete search or single-reviewer selection can undermine the entire review.
