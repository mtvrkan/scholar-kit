# Rubric: Theoretical & Conceptual Papers

---

## Dimension 1: Thesis Clarity & Significance (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the main argument clear? Is it significant or novel?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Thesis is stated explicitly and early (Introduction). Thesis is a clear position or argument (not just "exploring X"). Significance is evident (fills a gap in theory, challenges existing assumptions, offers new lens on known problem, provides synthesis of conflicting views). Reader understands immediately what claim is being made. |
| **4** | Thesis is clear and stated early. Significance is present. Some ambiguity in how novel the argument is. |
| **3** | Thesis is present but somewhat buried or vague. Significance is unclear or overstated. Reader must infer the main argument. |
| **2** | Thesis is vague, implicit, or appears late. Significance not justified. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No clear thesis. It's unclear what argument the paper is making. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Can you state the thesis in one sentence?
- Is the thesis a position (X is true because Y) or just a topic (Here are views on X)?
- Why does this thesis matter? (Addresses real gap? Challenges important belief? Offers practical insight?)

---

## Dimension 2: Argument Coherence & Logical Structure (1–5)

**Definition:** Are propositions logically connected? Does the argument build toward the thesis?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Main argument is laid out in Intro (thesis + roadmap). Each section advances the argument (Section 2 establishes X, Section 3 builds on X to argue Y). Conclusions follow from premises. No circular reasoning (assuming what you're trying to prove). Logical chain is transparent (if X then Y, and we've shown X, therefore Y). |
| **4** | Argument mostly coherent. Sections mostly advance thesis. Logic mostly transparent. |
| **3** | Argument present but loose. Some sections tangential. Logic sometimes implied or unclear. |
| **2** | Argument lacks clear structure. Sections don't obviously support thesis. Logical gaps present. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No discernible argument structure. Logical incoherence. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- If you remove any section, does the argument still hold?
- Are there tautologies (saying the same thing twice, claiming it as progress)?
- Does each section build on the previous, or are they standalone?
- Is the conclusion different from the premise, or just a restatement?

---

## Dimension 3: Evidence & Warrant – Claim-Ground Mapping (1–5)

**Definition:** Is each major claim supported by grounds (evidence or logic)? Is the reasoning (warrant) explicit?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Each major claim has explicit grounds: cited evidence, logical deduction, or empirical example. Warrant (the reasoning connecting grounds to claim) is articulated (not just implied). For each claim: "We know X (ground) because Y (warrant), which means Z (claim)." Alternative interpretations are acknowledged. |
| **4** | Most claims have supporting grounds. Warrant mostly explicit. Some alternatives mentioned. |
| **3** | Many claims have grounds, but warrant is often implicit. Reader must infer connections. Alternatives rarely mentioned. |
| **2** | Significant unsupported claims. Warrant is unclear or missing for key propositions. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Claims appear arbitrary or not grounded. Warrant is opaque. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- For the thesis, what evidence or logic supports it?
- Are there 1–2 claims that stand alone without support? (Red flag)
- For major theoretical claims, does the paper cite prior work or explain the reasoning?
- Does the author acknowledge "but some might argue X" for major claims?

---

## Dimension 4: Literature Review & Positioning (1–5)

**Definition:** How does this work relate to prior theory? Is positioning clear?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Relevant literature is cited and discussed accurately. For competing or foundational theories, the paper states their key claims and limitations. Paper's novel contribution relative to prior work is explicit (extends theory X, reconciles X and Y, offers alternative to X, etc.). Not just a list of citations; synthesis is evident. |
| **4** | Literature mostly covered. Key prior theories discussed. Positioning mostly clear. Synthesis present. |
| **3** | Literature review present but may miss key works. Positioning somewhat vague. Synthesis weak. |
| **2** | Literature review incomplete or inaccurate; misrepresents prior work. Positioning unclear. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Minimal literature review. Prior work misunderstood or ignored. Positioning unknown. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Would an expert in this subfield recognize all relevant prior work?
- Are citations accurate (you've read them, not secondhand)?
- For each prior theory, does the paper explain why it's incomplete or wrong (setting up the gap for this paper)?
- Is the paper's contribution explicit ("Unlike X, we argue that…")?

---

## Dimension 5: Consideration of Alternative Explanations & Counterarguments (1–5)

**Definition:** Does the paper engage seriously with opposing views? Are counterarguments addressed?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Major alternative explanations or competing theories are discussed. For each, the paper presents the strongest version (steel-manning, not strawman), then explains why it's insufficient or less plausible than the author's view. Reader understands the genuine debate. |
| **4** | Main counterarguments are addressed. Competing views presented fairly. Author's reasons for preferring their view are clear. |
| **3** | Some alternatives mentioned but not deeply engaged. Counterarguments briefly dismissed. |
| **2** | Counterarguments avoided or strawmanned (mischaracterized). Author's view presented as obviously correct. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No engagement with alternative views. Argument presented as unquestionable. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- If an expert disagreed with this paper, what would they say? Does the paper address that objection?
- Are competing theories presented in their strongest form, or weak versions?
- Does the paper say "Some argue X, but Y" (dismissive) or "Some argue X because of Z, which is a reasonable point, however…"?

---

## Dimension 6: Use of Examples & Illustrations (1–5)

**Definition:** Are abstract arguments grounded in examples? Are concepts clearly illustrated?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Major theoretical claims are illustrated with concrete examples or case studies. Figures (conceptual diagrams, taxonomy trees, process models) clarify complex ideas. Examples are varied and illuminating. Hypothetical scenarios show how theory applies in practice. |
| **4** | Examples and figures present. Mostly helpful and clear. |
| **3** | Some examples or figures present but sparse. Abstract sections lack illustration. |
| **2** | Few examples or figures. Dense abstract sections with no concrete grounding. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Completely abstract; no examples or illustrations. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- After reading a section, can you give an example of the theoretical concept explained there?
- Do figures add clarity, or are they ornamental?
- For novel theoretical terms, is an example given?

---

## Dimension 7: Falsifiability & Testability of Claims (1–5)

**Definition:** Are the paper's claims testable? Could evidence prove them wrong?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Major claims are stated in testable form (e.g., "If X, then Y should be observed" not just "X is important"). Paper suggests what evidence would support or refute claims (experiments, observations, etc.). Theory is falsifiable (not compatible with all possible outcomes). |
| **4** | Most claims are testable. Some refutation conditions suggested. Mostly falsifiable. |
| **3** | Claims are somewhat testable but vague. Refutation conditions not explicit. |
| **2** | Claims are stated in un-falsifiable form ("X is complex" or "many factors influence X"). No clear prediction. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Claims are circular or true by definition. Unfalsifiable. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- If someone designed an experiment based on this theory, what would they test?
- What empirical result would prove this paper's main claim wrong? (If nothing would, it's not a scientific claim)
- Are predictions specific (X causes Y to increase by ~5%) or vague (X makes Y better)?

---

## Dimension 8: Clarity & Accessibility of Writing (1–5)

**Definition:** Is the paper clearly written? Is jargon necessary and defined?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Writing is clear and precise. Jargon is minimized or necessary terms are defined on first use. Sentence length and structure aid understanding. Technical terms are used consistently. Abstract concepts are explained in plain language before formal definition. |
| **4** | Writing is mostly clear. Most jargon is defined. Occasional dense passages but generally accessible. |
| **3** | Writing is adequate but sometimes unclear or overly dense. Some jargon undefined. Reader must re-read paragraphs to understand. |
| **2** | Writing is hard to follow. Jargon is undefined or inconsistent. Excessive nominalization or nested clauses obscure meaning. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Writing is very poor. Incomprehensible passages. Inconsistent terminology. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Could a graduate student in the field (but new to this specific topic) understand the paper?
- Are technical terms explained, not just used?
- Is the writing concise (active voice, short sentences) or bloated (long nominalized phrases)?
- Are key concepts introduced before they're used?

---

## Dimension 9: Scope & Limitations Honesty (1–5)

**Definition:** Are scope limits acknowledged? Is generalizability claim appropriate?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Explicit limitations section: scope (e.g., "applies to Western industrialized societies"; "focuses on written text, not speech"), theoretical constraints (e.g., "assumes rational agents"), and unknowns (e.g., "role of emotion remains unclear"). Generalizability claim is modest and precise (e.g., "likely applicable to related contexts" not "true for all X"). |
| **4** | Limitations discussed. Scope mostly clear. Generalizability claim mostly appropriate. |
| **3** | Limitations mentioned but brief. Scope somewhat vague. Generalizability claim somewhat overstated. |
| **2** | Limitations vague or minimized. Scope limits not discussed. Generalizability overstated. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | No limitations. Generalizability claimed without justification. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Does the paper claim universal truth, or does it specify "under conditions X and Y"?
- Are contexts where the theory might NOT apply mentioned?
- Does the paper acknowledge what's unknown or unresolved?

---

## Dimension 10: Abstract ↔ Body Consistency (1–5)

**Definition:** Do abstract claims match body content and conclusion?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **5** | Abstract accurately summarizes thesis, main argument, and conclusion. Claims in abstract are directly supported in body. No overstatement. Tone matches (hedging language in abstract matches body). |
| **4** | Abstract mostly accurate and consistent. Minor discrepancies. |
| **3** | Abstract has some unsupported or vague claims. Reader must read full paper to verify. |
| **2** | Abstract makes claims not well-supported in body. Overstates conclusion. **[MAJOR]** |
| **1** | Abstract contradicts body. **[MAJOR]** |

**Reviewer checks:**
- Does every claim in the abstract appear in the main text with supporting argument?
- Does the abstract use hedging (suggested, likely) or absolute language (proven)?
- Is the conclusion in the abstract the same as the conclusion in the paper?

---

## Scoring Summary

**Publish:** Most dimensions ≥3; no Major issues.  
**Minor Revisions:** 1–2 Major issues (e.g., weak treatment of counterarguments, missing example). Fixable in 1 round.  
**Major Revisions:** 3+ Major issues or significant flaws (e.g., unsupported thesis, unfalsifiable claims, poor clarity).  
**Reject:** Thesis is unclear, argument is incoherent, or claims appear arbitrary/unfalsifiable.

---

## Reviewer Guidance

- **Theoretical work's strength** is in coherence, rigor of argument, and novel insight—not empirical results.
- **Red flag:** Unsupported claims (claim without warrant).
- **Red flag:** Unfalsifiable statements (claims that are true by definition or compatible with any outcome).
- **Strength:** Clear thesis, well-reasoned argument, acknowledges limitations and alternatives.
